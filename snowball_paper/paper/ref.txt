Snowball strategy -- reference reading list
=============================================
Each entry maps to ONE piece of the Snowball design (trend strength, momentum
strength, discount, dynamic leverage, pyramid scale-in, advantage-point entry,
dp/dv confirmation, limited-risk compounding) so they can be read
independently and combined afterward.

---

1. Strength of trend
---------------------
"Time Series Momentum"
Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen (2012), Journal of Financial Economics

Tests trend persistence (1-12 months) across 58 futures markets in equities,
currencies, commodities, bonds. Establishes that "has this asset been
trending" is a real, cross-asset, abnormal-return signal on its own -- the
core academic basis for the "strength of trend" leg of Snowball. Best during
extreme markets, which is directly relevant to why the strategy wants to be
able to lean in hard when a trend is confirmed.

- https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2089463
- https://w4.stern.nyu.edu/facdir/lpederse/papers/TimeSeriesMomentum.pdf


2. Strength of movement / momentum
------------------------------------
"Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers: Implications for Stock Market
Efficiency"
Jegadeesh & Titman (1993), The Journal of Finance

The original momentum paper: buying past 3-12 month winners and selling past
losers produces consistent forward returns. Read this for the "momentum" leg
specifically as magnitude-of-past-move, separate from trend-following's
time-series framing above -- useful for calibrating what counts as a
strong-enough move to trust.

- https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1993.tb04702.x
- https://www.bauer.uh.edu/rsusmel/phd/jegadeesh-titman93.pdf


3. Discount (distance from mean) -- two takes on the same idea
-----------------------------------------------------------------
"Price distance to moving averages and subsequent returns"
Alajbeg, Bubas & Vasic (2017), International Journal of Economics, Commerce
and Management

Simple, single-MA framing: the further price sits below (above) a moving
average, the more subsequent mean-reversion shows up. Closest match to
Snowball's literal "discount = price deviated from Fair Value/mean" concept.

- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353120123_Price_distance_to_moving_averages_and_subsequent_returns
- https://ijecm.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/5123.pdf

"Moving Average Distance as a Predictor of Equity Returns"
Avramov, Kaplanski & Subrahmanyam (2021), Review of Financial Economics

Uses the spread between a short and a long moving average (not price vs. one
MA) as the predictor -- structurally the same object as an EMA12-vs-EMA26
Action Zone distance, so this is worth reading specifically for how to turn
that spread into a sized signal rather than just a binary cross.

- https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/revfec/v39y2021i2p127-145.html


4. Dynamic leverage adjustment using volatility
---------------------------------------------------
"Volatility-Managed Portfolios"
Moreira & Muir (2017), The Journal of Finance

The direct academic source for exactly this phrase. Shows that scaling
exposure down when realized volatility is high (and up when it's low) raises
Sharpe ratios and produces large utility gains, across equities, value,
momentum, and other factors. This is the paper to read for the
mechanics/justification of regime-based dynamic leverage.

- https://amoreira2.github.io/alan-moreira.github.io/VolPortfolios_published.pdf
- https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2659431


5. Pyramid trading -- how to scale in
----------------------------------------
"Position Sizing in Trend-Following: Comparing Volatility Targeting,
Volatility Parity, and Pyramiding"
Concretum Group (practitioner research, not peer-reviewed, but directly
on-topic)

Compares three position-sizing engines head-to-head, one of which is
pyramiding: adding contracts as a trend extends by multiples of the initial
risk distance, sized so each layer contributes less risk than the one
before. The most direct source on frequency and sizing of pyramid adds
specifically.

- https://concretumgroup.com/position-sizing-in-trend-following-comparing-volatility-targeting-volatility-parity-and-pyramiding/


6. Entry at the advantage point (breakout from undervalue)
---------------------------------------------------------------
Turtle Trading System / Donchian Channel breakouts
Richard Dennis & William Eckhardt (1980s; widely documented, e.g.
"Research on Quantitative Trading Strategies Based on the Turtle Trading
Rule", ResearchGate, 2023)

Not a single peer-reviewed paper but the canonical source for "don't buy the
low, buy the breakout out of the base" -- a 20-day-high entry confirmed only
after price has already started moving, with ATR-based sizing. The
practitioner precedent for Snowball's "E1 explosion point" entry rule.

- https://www.researchgate.net/publication/370698720_Research_on_Quantitative_Trading_Strategies_Based_on_the_Turtle_Trading_Rule


7. dp + dv (delta price, delta volume)
------------------------------------------
"Price Momentum and Trading Volume"
Lee & Swaminathan (2000), The Journal of Finance

Studies the joint distribution of future returns conditional on both past
returns and past trading volume -- i.e., exactly the dp+dv pairing, not
price or volume in isolation. Findings: volume changes the interpretation of
a momentum signal (high-volume winners behave differently from low-volume
winners) -- the empirical basis for using volume as a confirmation filter
rather than a standalone signal.

- https://www.lsvasset.com/pdf/research-papers/Price-Momentum-Trad-Vol-2000.pdf


8. Limited risk, high return via compounding over distance/time
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Kelly Criterion / fractional Kelly literature
Start with Wikipedia's "Kelly criterion" for the map, then either the
original Kelly (1956) framing or the more recent "Optimal Betting: Beyond
the Long-Term Growth" (arXiv, 2025)

The formal treatment of "small linear risk per bet, geometric compounding
over many bets" -- the mathematical backbone of "limit risk, high return."
Fractional Kelly (half-Kelly, quarter-Kelly) is the standard practitioner
adjustment for model uncertainty, relevant to sizing a fixed initial risk
unit.

- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion
- https://arxiv.org/html/2503.17927v1


Bonus -- ties discount + momentum together as one system
=============================================================
"Value and Momentum Everywhere"
Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen (2013), The Journal of Finance

Shows value (~= "discount") and momentum are negatively correlated but both
independently profitable across eight markets -- empirical support for the
claim that discount alone isn't an entry signal and momentum alone isn't
either; you need both, and they complement rather than duplicate each other.
Good capstone read after the individual-factor papers above.

- https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~lpederse/papers/ValMomEverywhere.pdf
- https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2174501
